Little Kino

The programme, for grown-ups

  • A cartoon

    You sit and watch. It runs the same way every time, whether anyone is looking or not.

  • A game

    You play to win. There are points and levels, something to lose, and a reason to come back tomorrow.

  • A little world

    You visit. A dog in his yard, a mole in his meadow, one short day. Touch something and he does something with it. Nothing to win, and it ends when he goes to sleep.

See for yourself

This is Reksio. Tap him.

That is all a visit is made of. A child touches the bowl, the tree, the bird or the dog himself, and he answers, a little differently each time. A child who would rather watch can watch: he gets on with his day by himself.

A visit

One day, about {{DAY_MINUTES}} minutes.

  • {{DAY_FROM}}:00He wakes up and comes out.
  • All dayTap a place and he goes there. Tap a thing and he tries it. The weather turns, visitors come and go, the sun crosses the sky.
  • {{DAY_TO}}:00He is tired and goes to bed. That is the end.

The day is the same length for everyone. A child who watches a snail the whole time gets as long as one who tries everything, and nothing is lost when it ends.

How to play

Together, in the daytime.

  • Sit beside your child. There are no words on the screen, so the talking is yours: point, ask, laugh.
  • Not before bed. His bedtime is the end of his day, not a way to settle a child. Doctors advise no screens in the hour before sleep.
  • Turn the phone on its side. On an iPhone, add the front page to the Home Screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and it opens without the browser's bars.
  • When it is over, it is over. After he falls asleep, taps do nothing. To start a new morning, hold the small sun in the bottom-left corner for {{HOLD_SECONDS}} seconds. That is a grown-up's job: a toddler's tap can't do it.
  • Need to go? In Reksio's yard, hold his doghouse for {{HOLD_SECONDS}} seconds and the evening comes at once.
House rules

What it never does.

  • No ads, no purchases, no accounts.
  • No analytics: nothing about your child's play is collected.
  • No scores, no losing, nothing to collect.
  • No streaks, and no reward for playing longer or coming back.
  • No countdowns and no hurry.
  • No "play again". It doesn't start over by itself.
  • No words to read.
  • No claim that it teaches anything.

We read what doctors and researchers say about short screen play for toddlers before making this, and wrote down what follows from it. Read the research and our rules.

Where it comes from

Baja time.

Little Kino is made by a dad for his son Eliot, who is three and calls it baja time. Bajka is what a Polish child calls a cartoon. These are the ones his dad grew up with, from the years when a cartoon was a few minutes long and then it was over.

Each world is researched first: what the films show, who made them, how they sound, with a source for every claim. Then one home scene is built from it. The characters are drawn in code after the films, not traced from them. The sounds and music are new, made with a generative model and picked by ear. The one exception is Reksio's bark, which is his own, from the films.
